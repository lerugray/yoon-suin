## Scene Five: The Ward of the Salt Shore — The Miracle

You stand at the tidal mark in the yellow morning, twenty feet from an old crab-man who has spent twenty-two years tending offerings for a truth he was not allowed to keep. You look at the cracked shells arranged in their congregation-circles. You look at Krah-Moh.

You place your staff in the mud and open your hands.

*"Krah."*

He turns. His compound eyes find yours. You hold his gaze for a moment — long enough for him to understand this is not a direction but a request. Then you nod toward the old man under the arch.

You breathe. You reach for the miracle.

---

```
MIRACLE CAST — "Speak Truth to the Wretched"
Cost: 1 HP → Vothrog at 3/4 HP
Level 1, cost 1 — no save required; fires cleanly.
```

It is not a sound. It is not light. It is more like — pressure, the way truth feels when it is spoken in a room that has been waiting for it. The salt-flat air does not change. The yellow flowers at the tidal mark do not move. But something *opens*, the way a room opens when a window is found in a wall everyone agreed was solid.

You begin to speak. Not a sermon. Not a performance. You speak the way you spoke in the cellar at the dyewright's before the lanterns came, only quieter — the irreducible thing, stripped of rhetoric:

*"A crab-man came down the God River with nothing. He said: a being made property cannot have been made so by any god worth the name. He gathered people and they listened. The slug-lords called it insurrection and put it down. He was taken, and the workers kept coming to this waterline with shells and flowers because the truth does not stop being true when the person who said it is silenced."*

You stop.

*"I am not him. I am a slug-man, which is its own kind of obscenity in this context, and I know it. But I believe what he believed, and I am here, and I am not going away."*

You look at Krah-Moh.

He has already turned toward the old crab-man. His posture shifts — the weight moving differently through the great shell, the claws angling in ways you cannot fully read. He begins to click. Not the quick warning-clatter of startlement, not the stillness of watchfulness — something deliberate and measured, the cadence of someone speaking carefully in their own tongue for the first time in a long time.

The old crab-man in the archway has gone completely still. The click-speech from Krah-Moh moves through the salt air. The old man's compound eyes — ancient, worn, the facets dull with age — do not look at you while Krah-Moh speaks. They look at the tidal mark. At the offerings. At the white wave-scar on the left side of Krah-Moh's shell.

He sees that scar and something happens in his posture — a long, slow exhale of the whole body, twenty-two years of tension moving.

Then he rises. Slowly, with the careful deliberateness of very old joints. He walks to the tidal mark, not toward you, and he crouches at one of the offering-circles — one of the oldest ones, the shells bleached nearly white — and he touches it with one claw, flat, the same gesture Krah-Moh made when you arrived.

Then he turns and looks at you directly for the first time.

He clicks something. Once. Short. Final-sounding.

Krah-Moh goes very still. Then he turns to you and does something you have not seen him do before — he raises one claw, slowly, to the level of his own shoulder, and holds it there. An open claw, not defensive, not aggressive. Waiting.

You cannot speak crab-click language. You do not know what the old man said. But you know what an open hand looks like in every tongue.

From the old man's posture, from Krah-Moh's held claw, from the way the old man touched the oldest offering rather than the newest — you read this much:

He is not converted. He is not welcoming you into a fold. He is saying something more specific, something that Krah-Moh received as significant. The white scar on Krah-Moh's shell means something to him. He recognized it before he chose to rise.

And whatever he said — he is waiting for your response to it.
